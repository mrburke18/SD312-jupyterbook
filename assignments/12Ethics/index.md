# Ethical Essay

Every new powerful tool introduces both opportunities and risks, and,
therefore, ethical dilemmas.  Your assignment is to choose an ethical dilemma
related to the class material, and write an essay analyzing it. You should
cover the following things:
- What is the ethical issue? What is the source or cause of the circumstances
  which cause this issue? What is the problem that needs to be solved, or the
  possible harms that result from an unethical outcome? Not a bad idea to
  reference some article from a reputable source demonstrating your issue.
- What is, in your mind, the correct ethical stance to take? Use the [Moral Deliberation Roadmap](MoralDeliberationRoadmap.pdf) from your ethics core class to work through the dilemma and determine an ethical conclusion.
- What are the legitimate opposing viewpoints? What valid points do they
  raise, and why is your position more appropriate?
- Finally, argue an ethical *practical* position.  *Who* should be held responsible for doing *what* by *whom* in order to promote a more just outcome?  Justify how that mechanism will result in the desired outcome, and will be worth any negative repercussions.

You can write about whatever you like, but naturally, I get more excited reading a paper about a topic of interest to you, rather than one I brought up.

There is no page limit or requirement.  Make it however long it needs to be to clearly argue and justify your position, and no longer.

Every instructor looks for slightly different things in writing!  As fair warning, here's my pet peeves.

- Good spelling and grammar matter.  Poor spelling and grammar makes you come across as unprofessional and unclear.
- Unnecessary, word-padding cruft is very high school ("since the dawn of time, mankind has grappled with how to behave ethically...").  Clear communication requires that you get to the point, say what you need to say, and get out.
- Arguments need to be justified, either philosophically (like with the roadmap), or with citations.
- If you have citations, I do not care about the citation/bibliography format,
  as long as it's some clear and consistent format (not just a link).
- I'm a strong believer in starting with a plan, writing off that plan, and then editing.  It's usually possible to tell if a writer is figuring things out while they write, and it makes the writing less effective.

## Generative AI

Your Generative AI usage is limited. You are allowed to reference [this Google Gemini
Gem](https://gemini.google.com/gem/1C5UiKkTr_JF-DVF2cMvN97iyZILvyEIV?usp=sharing). This Gem is familiar with the assignment and the moral
deliberation roadmap. You should attach a pdf of your paper and set it to "Thinking,"
rather than "Fast." You must use the following verbatim prompt:

> Attached is a rough draft of a paper on an ethical issue in machine learning.
> I have been asked to do the following:
>
> - Use proper spelling and grammar,
> - Express myself in a precise and concise manner, without unnecessary filler,
> - Justify my arguments, either philosophically or with citations,
> - Acknowledge the good points from legitimate opposing positions but argue why my position is better,
> - Take a practical position, with specific suggestions for who should be held responsible for doing what, by whom, in order to promote a more just outcome.
>
> Critique my paper in these areas. You may suggest specific spelling or
> grammar changes, but otherwise do not suggest any text changes that can be
> copy-pasted wholesale - instead, point out problematic portions of the paper
> where I could improve in these areas.

You are free to consider or ignore the feedback from generative AI. It is
quite plausible that the advice from the generative AI is wrong or unhelpful,
so you should take it or leave it as you consider best.

Write your paper in Google Docs, and share it with your instructor so he can
see the version history.
